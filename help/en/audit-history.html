Review 4: Evolution according to Git

1,495 commits in 80 days and a 10 series that changed the architecture in a week. Updated on 13 November 2026.

Method and limits

Facts are checked with git log, git show and git log -S; dates and versions are those of each commit. Interpretations are marked as such. The phases before 9.11.0 are summarized; the detail of this review is in the 10.x series.

Dates are author dates: some commits after 9.11.0 keep earlier dates because the branch was reorganized.

1. Figures

  • 1,495 commits between 26 August and 13 November 2026, with activity on 68 of those 80 days.
  • 251 commits since 9.11.0 (170ee03), with 19 closed milestones (M44 to M62) and more than 20 versions: 9.11.1 to 9.11.5 and 10.0.0 to 10.14.0.
  • Fifteen minor versions of the 10 series in seven days (7 to 13 November), all publishable on PyPI.
  • The pace speeds up at the end: 21, 33, 43 and 27 commits from 10 to 13 November.
  • Of the 251 commits, 115 declare a co-author: 95 Claude Opus 5.5, 13 Claude Fable 5.2 and 7 Claude Sonnet 5.5. The other 136 declare none.
  • In code, tests and scripts: 133 files changed, 14,635 lines added and 4,795 removed.
  • The suite went from 702 cases to 809; the backend from 4,909 to 7,995 lines.

2. Phases of evolution

From ChatCLI to 9.11 (26 August – 24 October)

It is born as ChatCLI v0.9 (39fed30), is renamed ZeroChat, adopts AGENTS.md on 2 September, drops the bundle in 7.0.0 and does its great cleanup in 9.0.0, removing 16,500 lines. The 9 series adds office documents, its own web transport, profiles, Playwright, unattended execution with a browser and, in 9.11.0, the remote connection with sign-in through the system account.

Pause and maintenance (25 October – 5 November)

Five fix versions (9.11.1 to 9.11.5) and ten days with very little activity. WebP images start being sent as JPEG (M44).

The server stores data (6 – 7 November, 10.0.0)

In two days SQLite appears in the backend: profiles (M45, 9cbbd8f), conversations (M46) and background turn execution with --conversation (M47, 0a4df3b). It is the largest architectural change since 7.0.0: until then the server stored nothing belonging to the user.

Consolidating server mode (7 – 9 November, 10.1 – 10.6)

A separate attachment store to stay under the 1 MiB limit per request (M49), the profile remembered per conversation (M50), server profiles separated from local ones, without the Z- prefix introduced four days earlier (M51), a more robust web transport (M52) and another test pruning (M53).

Permissions move to the backend (10 – 12 November, 10.7 – 10.12)

M56 moves authorization to the server and removes the HMAC signature from the client. M60 removes permission evaluation from the frontend. M61, with 39 tasks in two days, rebuilds the integrity of the system: single-use challenges, SQLite as the only persistence, per-argument command patterns and subcommand-by-subcommand validation. It started from a specification written beforehand (docs/audits/M61-permisos.md).

Node replaces the browser in runs (10 – 11 November, 10.8 – 10.10)

M57 swaps Chromium for a Node.js process for --prompt and --conversation (1fb6441), with the runner published on GitHub Pages. M58 and M59 add execute_javascript with node:vm (9685350) and render_chart the next day.

Multi-session by project (12 November, 10.13)

M62 defines "session = project": the process stops changing directory, each request carries its project, and session permissions, challenges, commands and browsers are separated. Multi-user is decided to live outside the process and is only documented.

Adjusting with measurements (13 November, 10.14)

M63 measures the history with 10,000 conversations before deciding (8edaade), fixes the sidebar painting and synchronizes headers only; it then simplifies AgentRuntime, ToolExecutor and ConversationService by removing parameters and guards with no consumer.

Interpretation: the 9.0.0 lesson holds wherever a rule is written: it arrives with its test the same day or the next. The gaps of the 10 series are mostly of a different kind. They are not rules without a test but guarantees nobody got to write down, because the feature shipped in one or two days: that the sandbox isolates, that the runner is current, that deleting frees space. The exception is the rotation of session permissions: written in the README and tested only at startup.

4. What history teaches

  • Specifying first pays off. M61 (permissions) and M62 (multi-session) started from a specification and recorded decisions; they are the best-covered areas of the series.
  • Measure before limiting. M63 measured with 10,000 conversations and found the bottleneck was not the assumed one: the sidebar, not the database.
  • What ships in a day is redone within a week. The Z- profile prefix lasted four days; the client HMAC signature, removed in M56, still leaves traces; permission evaluation in the frontend was written and removed within the same series.
  • Each new environment doubles the guarantees to test. The browser sandbox had its security tests; taking it to Node copied the interface, not the level of isolation.
  • Continuous cleanup works. M53 and M63 removed tests and code with no consumer a few days after they were written, without waiting for a great cleanup like that of 9.0.0.

Interpretation: the project has shown it can change architecture in a week without breaking the suite. The risk is no longer accumulating old debt, but speed leaving guarantees unstated in the newest parts.
